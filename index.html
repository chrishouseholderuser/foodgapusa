THE FOOD GAP · UNITED STATES

Which American communities face the greatest risk of food insecurity?

When a household's income falls too low, reliable access to enough healthy food is often the first thing to go. Low income is the strongest predictor of household food insecurity in the United States.

This map shows 2,083 U.S. counties that contain food-desert tracts — not every U.S. county — shaded by a custom severity index from USDA Food Access Research Atlas (2019) tract data.

METHODOLOGY

How this map measures food-desert severity

What is shown

This map covers 2,083 U.S. counties (50 states + DC) where the USDA Food Access Research Atlas flags low-income / low-access (“food desert”) census tracts. Counties with no desert tracts in the source file are not drawn. Shading uses a custom severity index (0–100) built from Atlas tract indicators — not a single published USDA county LILA percentage.

People and stores

County population and store counts come from Census and Food Environment Atlas fields packaged with the map. “People affected” aggregates desert-related population fields from the pipeline; treat totals as approximations.

Severity levels

Minor → Critical bands follow the index cutoffs in the packaged data (roughly minor 7–38 · moderate 39–48 · severe 48–59 · critical 59–94). The numeric score is always shown in the detail panel.

Urban / rural

Urban vs rural is a derived density flag from the source package, not a Census Urban Area designation.

Grocery spending

All dollar amounts are U.S. dollars (USD). Weekly grocery figures are modelled estimates from BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey national food-at-home spending, scaled by county income — not local receipts. The fruits-&-vegetables share is a national constant in this edition.

Limitations

  • Severity is a custom Atlas-based index, not an official USDA county LILA rate.
  • Not every U.S. county appears — only counties with desert tracts in the packaged data (e.g. NYC borough counties are absent).
  • Weekly grocery and produce share are modelled / national, not store-level prices.
  • County geometry is generalized for the web.
  • This is an independent visualization, not an official USDA, CDC, Census, or BLS product.
